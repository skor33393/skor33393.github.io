Can pelvic floor exercises help diastasis recti?

An abdominal gap and pelvic floor symptoms need a coordinated plan; a Kegel routine alone is not a complete recovery programme.

By Pelvic GymNovember 11, 20264 min read

Educational content · Our editorial standards · Sources linked below

The short answer

Pelvic floor exercises can be one part of recovery, but diastasis recti concerns the abdominal wall. The NHS describes pelvic floor and deep abdominal exercises together as potentially helpful. Get an individual assessment for an ongoing gap, pain or difficulty with daily tasks instead of assuming that more Kegels will solve the problem.

What to take away

  • Abdominal separation and pelvic floor weakness are different findings.
  • Discuss how movement feels as well as the size of the gap.
  • A guided Kegel app cannot assess the abdominal wall or supply a full diastasis rehabilitation plan.

What is diastasis recti?

Diastasis recti is separation of the paired rectus abdominis muscles at the front of the abdomen. The NHS explains that this commonly develops during pregnancy as the growing uterus stretches the abdominal area.

A pelvic floor exercise targets a different muscle group. Having an abdominal gap does not, by itself, tell you how well your pelvic floor contracts or relaxes. Tell your clinician about leakage, heaviness, pain or emptying difficulties separately rather than treating the gap as an explanation for every symptom.

Why are both kinds of exercise discussed?

NHS post-pregnancy guidance includes pelvic floor and deep abdominal exercises as parts of recovery that may help reduce separation. That is broader than a programme consisting only of repeated Kegels.

Cleveland Clinic recommends working with a physiotherapist experienced in diastasis recti to choose movements, check technique and progress appropriately. A useful plan should explain the purpose of each exercise and how it relates to the activity you want to regain.

For example, ask which practice is intended for bladder control, which is intended for abdominal function, and how to coordinate them. If pelvic floor contractions are painful or you struggle to release, discuss that before adding strengthening.

What should I take to the appointment?

Bring your birth and recovery history, current exercise instructions and one or two ordinary tasks that are difficult. You do not need a polished description or a particular gap measurement to ask for help.

This is a conversation checklist, not a diagnostic test. A photo, a finger-width estimate and an exercise app cannot replace an examination of what is happening during your own movement.

When should I ask for help?

The NHS advises contacting a GP if an abdominal gap remains obvious eight weeks after birth, and seeking help at any time for physical problems that bother you. Eight weeks is a prompt for review, not a deadline by which everyone must recover.

Do not assume persistent abdominal pain is caused by separation. Use the postpartum guide's advice on recurrent pain and its urgent warning signs. Seek care sooner for concerning symptoms rather than waiting for an exercise trial to end.

At a later stage, you can still ask for an assessment. Explain what remains difficult now instead of judging your situation against someone else's early postpartum photos.

Agree what progress means for you

A practical review might include carrying your usual shopping more comfortably or getting through a chosen daily activity with better control. Ask your clinician which observations and measurements are useful for your case, how often to review them and what should trigger an earlier call.

Keep a short record of the plan you actually followed and the task you wanted to improve. For instance: “I practiced the prescribed movement, but standing up while holding the baby still feels difficult.” That gives the clinician a specific problem to reassess. It is more useful than silently increasing repetitions because a mirror image looks unchanged.

Avoid turning recovery into a requirement to achieve a flat abdomen. The exercise decision should serve function, comfort and your own priorities.

Can Pelvic Gym be part of the plan?

If your clinician includes pelvic floor strengthening, Pelvic Gym can support that part with guided workouts. Keep the abdominal exercises and any restrictions from your clinician alongside it. The app does not measure separation, assess doming or replace an individual abdominal rehabilitation programme.

For broader recovery questions, read our postpartum guide. For help finding a suitable service, use the pelvic health physiotherapy checklist.

Common questions

Are Kegels alone a complete diastasis recti programme?

No. A pelvic floor routine does not assess or train every part of abdominal recovery. Ask for a plan that addresses your abdominal function and any pelvic floor symptoms.

Do I need to wait eight weeks before asking for help?

No. Ask for help whenever a physical problem bothers you. The NHS eight-week advice is a reason to review a persistent gap, not a rule to delay care.

Your next step

If you have pain, pressure, or trouble relaxing, a pelvic health professional can help you work out what your muscles need. An exercise app cannot make that assessment.
