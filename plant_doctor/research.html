Research & design decisions

Reviewed 25 October 2026. The landing-page promise is deliberately broad: scan a plant, understand the problem, and get a care plan. Yellow leaves are a visible example, not the product’s entire proposition.

These are observable design patterns from established app and campaign pages, not a verified ranking of their conversion performance. Their private conversion rates and acquisition economics are unknown.

Five hypotheses

  1. Instant Answer: an immediately readable result can make the utility clear.
  2. Scan Lab: a dark AI-focused presentation can make scanning feel advanced.
  3. Plant SOS: a warm, direct symptom-to-answer story can lower hesitation.
  4. The Care Plan: practical steps may be a stronger hook than diagnosis alone.
  5. Field Notes: a quieter editorial approach may feel more trustworthy.

Rules carried over from the Stylist AI work

Show the core result immediately. Use recognizable product visuals and consistent feature cards. Keep store badges visible. Avoid redundant navigation, secondary “Explore” buttons, FAQ filler, and fabricated social proof. Check small phones and browser-height changes. Record traffic sources, scroll, feature views, viewing time, and CTA placement.

The selected icon is the exact green two-leaf-and-plus artwork supplied by the user. It is used consistently in all five layouts. The original ad footage is reused; no new videos were generated for these pages.

What this test can establish

Store-button taps measure stated download intent after seeing a concept. They do not establish installs, willingness to pay, diagnostic accuracy, retention, or plant recovery. The pages label the scans as examples and explain that the app is coming soon after a tap.
