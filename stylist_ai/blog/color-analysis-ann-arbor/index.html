Ann Arbor color analysis: make a two-hour consultation useful afterwards

Three blanks to fill during the session

  • My easiest everyday neutral is …
  • I can combine that neutral with …
  • The makeup change I want to try first is …

Use these as your own notes; they are not promised outputs beyond the provider’s described package.

What a two-hour appointment should help you take home

A two-hour Ann Arbor consultation gives you time for more than a quick season label, but its value still depends on what you want to learn. Decide whether your priority is an everyday wardrobe, a specific event outfit or makeup shades. A useful reference is one you can understand without the analyst sitting beside you.

Prepare examples, not a complete closet inventory

Bring a small number of questions, not your entire closet. Ask the consultant to show why one neutral works better for your intended look and how to pair it with an accent. Find out how to use the take-home swatch with printed fabrics and what the booklet explains. Leave time to write down the combinations you would actually repeat.

  • What does the booklet explain beyond my season name?
  • What happens to the deposit if I need to change the appointment?

Read your booklet before the shopping trip

After the visit, make a three-outfit test from existing clothes. Keep the silhouettes familiar and change only one color at a time. That makes it easier to decide whether the advice helps rather than attributing every difference to the season label. A phone palette can stay available during shopping, while the physical swatch can help you compare the cloth itself.

Keep practice separate from the appointment

Two hours with an analyst and a booklet to take away offer an opportunity for questions. Make use of that by asking for an explanation you could repeat to a friend, rather than collecting color names. The listed cancellation terms refer to a deposit and a 24-hour window; reread the booking policy before reserving a time.
