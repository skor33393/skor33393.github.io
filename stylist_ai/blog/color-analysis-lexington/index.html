Use color advice before a hair appointment

Lexington readers considering a new hair color have a practical reason to explore personal color analysis before booking the change. Choosing a flattering direction and carrying out a salon treatment are separate steps. A color consultation can help frame the first discussion without providing a formula for the second.

Book the color discussion by its actual service name

Bring photos of your natural hair, current hair and two or three looks you are considering. Ask whether the consultation covers clothing and makeup as well as hair, and whether a separate salon appointment is needed. Your stylist should assess your current color history, condition and maintenance preferences before recommending a process. Avoid booking a complicated change solely from a palette name.

  • Is hair coloring booked and charged separately from analysis?
  • Can the consultation help me compare specific hair references?

Make a direction board, not a chemical formula

Stylist AI can help you compare a hair-color preview with your usual clothing colors before the appointment. Save the looks you enjoy and explain what appeals to you: depth, softness or contrast. Keep one familiar makeup look while comparing, so you are not changing every feature at once. A useful outcome is a short list to discuss with the salon, not an instruction to bleach or dye at home.

Use previews to explain what you mean

Words such as richer, softer or brighter can mean different things to a client and a colorist. A few visual references make the conversation easier. The LUX page directs visitors to book by phone and treats hair appointments separately; the reviewed text did not establish a current analysis fee.

Stylist AI’s hair previews can help you explore those visual directions, while its seasonal palette, outfit combinations and lip or blush previews show related ideas. The starting flow uses your own selfie. These tools are for considering a look; they do not assess your hair’s condition or prescribe a salon formula. Bring the directions you prefer to an in-person conversation if you are deciding on a treatment.
