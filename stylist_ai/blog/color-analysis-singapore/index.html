App or in-person color analysis?

Choose by the kind of help you want. Both routes can be useful; they provide different experiences.

A photo and a physical drape are different inputs. Lighting and camera processing can change how colors appear; a season name is a styling reference, not a rule for every purchase.

Compare individual attention, not just the group total

Colour analysis in Singapore is available through studio consultations as well as phone apps. The useful distinction is how you want to explore colour: physical draping and a live explanation, or a self-guided palette and photo tools you can revisit. A studio’s location, consultation language and individual time matter as much as the season system.

A lower per-person price does not mean a longer individual consultation. Ask how the shared time is divided, what materials each person receives and whether makeup discussion is included. Compare the same deliverables before calling one package better value.

Confirm language, materials and the complete fee

Ask which language the consultant will use for the entire explanation, not just the booking conversation. If you want English or Mandarin guidance, confirm that directly. Bring a short list of the clothes and cosmetics you want help with, and ask whether the session includes product review, application, a lesson or only colour recommendations.

Check whether the quoted amount includes any applicable tax, a physical palette and digital notes. For a group, confirm the cancellation terms if one person cannot attend. Avoid assuming that a short demonstration or event package is equivalent to a full private consultation.

  • How much draping and discussion does each guest receive?
  • Which palette or written notes can I keep afterwards?
  • Can the consultant help with the items and language I need?

Use the palette for your actual daily routine

Choose one everyday outfit and one work or occasion outfit to practise with. If you move between outdoor daylight and indoor shopping lights, compare the garment in more than one setting before buying. Keep texture, coverage and comfort in the decision: a colour palette does not choose the right fabric for you.

Start with a useful neutral, an accent and a lip colour from things you already own. If you wear a head covering, compare the fabric nearest your face rather than assuming hair colour must be visible for every styling choice. Discuss your preferences with a consultant and keep the colours you enjoy.

Stylist AI is available in the Singapore App Store. You can begin with a selfie in even light, explore a personal palette and preview makeup colours. A studio adds physical draping and human discussion; an app offers a way to keep experimenting on your phone. Use whichever helps the decision in front of you, without treating either route as a reason to replace your whole wardrobe.

A useful first-week plan

  1. Pick a familiar outfit and keep the shape and makeup consistent.
  2. Compare one neutral and two accents instead of replacing the whole outfit.
  3. Try a favorite combination on an ordinary day, then record what you liked.
