# Daily Google Trends research

Authorized 2026-11-04 as an addition to the existing 22:00 Asia/Dubai workflow. Starting with the next scheduled run, check recent rising searches for both apps and aim to include one qualified trend-led question per app within the existing five. Keep the other question sources and all publication, clinical, design-approval and advertising protections. This does not create a second schedule or rerun a completed daily batch.

## Check and record

1. Use the official Google Trends Explore interface or an available official connector. Start with Web Search, All categories, United States and Canada separately, Past 7 days. Use `color analysis` and `pelvic floor` as baseline seeds. Supplement with relevant seeds such as `seasonal color analysis`, `outfit colors`, `kegel exercises` or `pelvic floor exercises` when the baseline misses the reader's intent; log all seeds and whether each is a search term or a selected topic. Do not infer a topic identifier or silently combine languages.
2. Inspect Related queries set to Rising and the corresponding Top queries. Expand to Past 30 days when data is sparse or a potentially useful candidate needs context. Record the period actually available; a 30-day increase is not evidence of a surge today. For a promising seasonal query, inspect 12 months for recurring interest. Use worldwide only as a labeled fallback when regional data is insufficient. These are research scopes, not authorization for more city pages or translated articles.
3. Optionally inspect Trending Now for a relevant current event. That feed often concerns news and does not replace niche Explore checks. Ignore irrelevant matches and competitor navigation without a useful independent reader decision. Stop on rate limits or access challenges; record the limit and continue the other authorized research sources. Never bypass restrictions or substitute an unofficial scraping endpoint.
4. Record the observation time, Explore URL, seed/type, country, category, search type, exact dates or displayed relative range, current and comparison windows when exposed, query text, Rising percentage or Breakout label, Top score if observed, and data limitations. Save the evidence in the date-specific run under `google_trends`; raw exports/screenshots belong outside the publishing repository. Preserve unknowns as null. Do not advance the Reddit/Quora scan watermark because of a Trends check.

## Choose a useful question

For each app, shortlist relevant candidates before ranking them. Inspect actual Google results and People also ask for the strongest candidates to establish what the searcher wants; record location/language and access limits. If wording is turned into an article question, retain both the observed query and the editorial question. Check all existing journal and city intents before selecting a destination.

Prioritize a clear reader decision, evidence of a recent rise, an answer we can substantiate, and a specific appropriate app role. Consider data sufficiency and corroboration rather than picking the largest percentage automatically. Reject unrelated associations, ambiguous or unsupported medical claims, and one-off sparse-data spikes. For health topics, Trends establishes interest only; verify advice against authoritative clinical sources and preserve assessment-first guidance and honest review status.

An overlapping query leads to a material improvement of the existing article with its original publication date. A distinct intent can become a new article using the existing approved template, useful original example/resource, relevant incoming links and install CTA. Do not publish an empty update solely to claim trend coverage. If none qualifies, record `no_suitable_trend`, `insufficient_data` or `unavailable` with reasons and continue regular question-led publication. A missing trend slot is not automatically an overall article shortfall.

Record each candidate's overlap destination, reader decision, original resource, app role, selection/rejection reason and outcome (`new`, `update`, `skip`). Report a selected trend's article link and brief reason, including its country and period if mentioning a growth figure. Keep original forum discussions out of public pages and routine notifications.

## Interpret carefully

- Explore interest scores are normalized 0–100, not monthly search volume or site visitors. Scores from independently scaled charts are not directly comparable.
- Rising percentages compare growth with the previous period. Breakout means growth over 5,000%; it does not establish a large audience. Preserve the label rather than inventing an exact percentage.
- Low-volume zeros may mean insufficient data. One-off spikes can include statistical noise. Partial current periods must not be compared as if complete.
- A related query is discovery evidence; it is not automatically relevant, a literal reader question, a clinical source or a ranking opportunity.
- Compare post-publication Search Console impressions/clicks and consented analytics over equal reported periods; a trend-led article is an experiment, not a ranking or traffic guarantee.
